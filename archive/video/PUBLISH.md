One video of a room goes in. An adjustable 3D room comes out.

Movable Reality turns a walk through a room into a 3D Gaussian Splatting scene where each piece of furniture is its own object. Select, move, turn and measure things in the browser, add furniture from a library, or tell an agent what you want. After every edit, a second model looks at the room and checks the result.

No depth sensor, no floor plan, no hand labels. It takes the video, the focal length and the ceiling height, and one command runs the whole pipeline.

It is a working demo on two rooms rendered in Blender, and a good starting point for a project or research. It is MIT licensed, each stage is a plain script you can swap, and the test rooms and scoring tools are included so you can measure your changes.

Eight Meta models
Muse Wonder: lists the objects in the video and edits the scene
SAM 3.1: finds and follows every object through the video
MapAnything: cameras and geometry from the frames
SAM 3D Objects: builds the hidden side of each object
Muse Image: new furniture to insert, and repairs to thin spots
Muse Spark Open: looks at a screenshot after every edit
Muse Voice Transcribe: voice commands
Muse Spark: short helper calls

Rendering uses open-source code with no learned weights: gsplat, Spark and three.js.

Built by team Larracat for the Meta Global AI Developer Hackathon, 26 Oct to 4 Nov 2026. The music was written in code for this video.
